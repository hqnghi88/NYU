In both case studies, the overwhelming volume of mixed vehicles consistently results in systemic congestion of both cars and motorbikes. The solution we propose is implementing a digital twin to model the segregation of vehicles with designated lanes for cars and motorbikes at urban intersections to measure how many vehicles can successfully pass through a junction within a given time period. The goal of this solution is to alleviate extreme congestion and improve traffic flow efficiency in high-density environments. With this model, we hope to see an increase in the number of vehicles that can pass through each intersection at a given time. Using the digital twin model, we simulate four stages of public compliance, as we observed that many people in Vietnam do not follow traffic guidelines. We will gradually reduce rule-breaking behavior from 80% to 20% to evaluate how both infrastructure design and human behavior influence intersection throughput. We hypothesize that segregated lanes will reduce conflict points and inefficient merging, enabling higher vehicle throughput even when compliance is low. Overall, this study aims to determine whether infrastructure-driven solutions can effectively counter behavioral unpredictability and increase intersection capacity without relying solely on 100% enforcement of human behavior.
